How to use Morse Key

Morse Key has one button. You tap and hold it to send dots and dashes, and the page turns them into letters. Learn teaches you the code. Chat lets you talk to friends with it.

The key

  1. Tap for a dot. A quick press is a dot.
  2. Hold for a dash. While you hold, the label on the key says DOT. When you have held long enough it changes to DASH. Let go when it shows the one you want.
  3. Pause to lock in a letter. Stop for about half a second after your last press. The letter appears on screen and in the big letter beside the key.
  4. Pause longer for a space. Wait about a second and a space is added between words.
  5. Listen and watch. Every press plays a beep and lights the green TX lamp. Incoming sound lights the amber RX lamp.
A = dot, dash

A dash is held about three times as long as a dot. On a computer you can hold the space bar instead of clicking the key.

Speed. SLOW, MID and FAST sit beside the key. They set how long you hold for a dash and how long the pauses are. Start on SLOW and move up when it feels easy. The sound button turns the beeps on or off.

Learn

Free key is for exploring. Key any letter and watch the chart: the path you took glows green and the spot you are on glows orange. Your letters collect on the green screen. Tap a letter on the chart to see its pattern and hear it.

Reading the chart. Start at the funnel. A dot goes left and a dash goes right. A dot then a dash lands on A.

Train gives you one letter at a time.

  1. Pick a level. Each level adds new letters.
  2. Key the big letter or word shown on screen. For a word, key one letter at a time and pause between letters. Each correct letter turns green.
  3. If you are right, the letter turns green and the next one appears. If you are wrong, you see what you keyed next to the right pattern.
  4. Press Listen to hear the letter. Press Show hint to see its dots and dashes. After two misses the hint opens by itself.
  5. A letter counts as learned after three correct answers, a word or phrase after two. Progress is saved on this device.

Chat

  1. Open the Chat tab. Type your name and a room code, or press New room to get a code.
  2. Press Join. The status shows LIVE when you are connected.
  3. Give the room code to your friends. They open this same page, enter the same code and press Join. Their names appear under the room code.
  4. Choose Audio or Text at the top of the room.

Audio. Hold the key to talk. Your friends hear each beep the moment you press it, just like a telegraph. Their beeps play on your device the same way, and the line under the switch shows who is keying. Everyone needs the page open and sound on. Each friend has their own pitch, so you can tell voices apart.

Text. Key a message and the letters appear on the green screen. To send, key AR () or press Send. To erase the last letter, key eight dots or press Erase. Each message is sent as recorded audio. Friends see it as a strip of dots and dashes, and it plays as beeps when it arrives. Press PLAY to hear it again. Press TEXT to read the message as letters, and HIDE to put it away. A dot beside Text means a new message arrived while you were in Audio.

Who can chat. Anyone with the site link and the same room code. Room codes are not secret, so pick one that is hard to guess when you want a private chat.

Audio and messages are live only. Friends get what is sent while they are in the room, and nothing is stored. If the status shows LOCAL, there is no live connection and messages stay on this device.

If something is off

  • No sound. Check that Sound is on and your phone is not on silent. Tap the key once so the browser allows audio.
  • Dashes come out as dots. Hold a little longer, or switch to SLOW.
  • Letters split in two. You paused too long in the middle. Keep going at an even pace, or switch to SLOW for longer gaps.
  • A question mark appears. The pattern does not match a letter. Wait for it to lock in and try again.
  • Chat says LOCAL. The live connection did not open. Check your internet connection, then leave the room and join again.

Tips

  • Think in rhythm. Morse code has a musical rhythm. Think of A (·−) as "di-dah", not "dot dash". Your taps will flow naturally.
  • Sound is essential. Always use the audio. Professional operators read Morse by ear, not by eye. Keep the volume up and let the beeps guide you.
  • Learn the sound of each letter, not the count of dots and dashes. Use Listen often.
  • Five short sessions a day work better than one long one.
  • Finish a level before you start the next. Chat is easier once the first two levels feel natural.
